# The word is the mark.
Rabta has no symbol. It is written: five rounded lowercase letters as one
outlined path, in one colour. This page is the wordmark: the two grounds
it sits on, the tile, the rules, and the files to use it.
## One path, one colour.
The wordmark is a single outlined path, so it never depends on a font
being installed and never changes shape between machines. It takes
the text colour of whatever it sits on: ink on paper, paper on ink.
The ember is never on the letters.

## Rabta is رابطة: a bond, a link, the thing that ties things together.
The product is exactly that. Your tools each remember their own piece
of a task; Rabta is the bond between them. The name is pronounced
RAHB-tah. In running text it is a word like any other; the wordmark
is for the places the brand signs its name.

## Ink on paper, paper on ink, and the tile.
Two colourways and nothing in between: ink on a paper surface, paper
on an ink surface. Inlined, the wordmark takes the text colour on its
own. The tile is the ink wordmark on an ember squircle, for the Dock,
the favicon and any avatar: the one place the ember and the letters
share a frame, and even there the letters stay ink.

## Clear space, and the smallest it goes.
Keep one cap height of clear space on every side: the height of the
b, from its top to the baseline. Nothing else sits inside that box.
Never set the wordmark below 22px tall, and never the tile below
16px. Under those, write the name.
The dashed line is the clear space: the wordmark, plus one cap height on every side.
22px, the floor 16px, the tile's floor
## Never.
Outline it
The letters are filled shapes. A stroke around them is a different, thinner word.
Rotate it
It reads on a baseline, and only there.
Stretch it
Scale both axes together, always. The proportions are the wordmark.
Put it in a circle
The tile is a squircle, and the tile is the only container it has.
Add a shadow
Or a glow, a gradient, or a second colour. It is flat, in one colour, on a flat surface.
Set the name in a typeface instead
Where the wordmark belongs, use the path. Type is for the name in running text.
## Paper, ink, one ember.
Both worlds are neutral with a cool cast, so a screenshot of the dark
app sits naturally on the light site. The ember is spent once per
view: on the primary action, or on the live thing, never both, and
never on the wordmark.

## Everything that comes back settles the same way.
One settling curve for anything that comes to rest, and one spring
for the moment something lands. The wordmark arrives in just over a
second: it rises into place and settles on the spring. A window
returns by rising into place; a headline rises from behind its own
baseline; a restore completes with the ember. Under reduced motion,
all of it collapses to a fade, and the wordmark is simply there.
