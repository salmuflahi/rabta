The whole product

Three moves.
Nothing else to learn.

Capture a task when you stop. Switch away to anything. Resume it when you come back, and the editor files, terminals, browser tabs and branch return with it.

One keystroke

Saving it is the whole ritual.

Press Capture and Rabta writes down the arrangement around the task — the open files and their order, the terminal directories, the branch, the tab addresses. It records where things were, never what was in them.

A return you can trust

The return, shown honestly.

Rabta restores what it can now and tells you what is waiting on another app. A partial result is not hidden in a footnote.

Focus mode · off by default

Resuming can also put away what isn’t in the task.

Restoring has always been additive: it opens things and closes nothing, so three resumed tasks leave three projects open at once. Turn focus mode on and resuming clears the rest away first.

It stops at anything you’d lose
Never closes unsaved files, pinned tabs, or terminals that are still running something.
Nothing is lost
Rabta saves the outgoing task before it switches, so anything put away comes back by resuming that task. There is no separate undo, because going back is the undo.

History

Every task you came back to.

Capsules are not a log you have to keep. They accumulate on their own, and the ones you stop resuming quietly stop mattering.

Local-first

There is no account, because there is no server.

Capsules are files on your disk. Rabta has no backend to send them to, no telemetry, and nothing to sign in to. The hub binds 127.0.0.1 and nothing else; the browser connector reads tab addresses and titles, and cannot read a page. Delete the app and the data goes with it.
